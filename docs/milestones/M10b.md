# M10b: flyovers

Goal: the player can build a flyover at a junction. Two opposite roads are lifted over the junction, so through traffic between them bypasses it completely, and everything else still uses the junction.

This is the first edit that appends roads and nodes, so it also makes road geometry editable while keeping every id stable. Undo truncates the appended ids.

This implements plan §3.5 ("Id stability") and the `BuildFlyover` row of §3.6.

## Geometry becomes range-based (`network/road.rs`)

- `RoadStore.point_start: Vec<u32>` becomes `point_range: Vec<(u32, u32)>`, which is `(start, len)`.
- `points` and `cumulative` stay append-only.
- Re-pointing a road to new geometry appends the new points and updates its range. Old points become unreferenced garbage, which is accepted because edits are rare.
- `points(road)` and `cumulative(road)` read through the range.
- `length: Vec<f64>` stays a column, because `setback.rs` and `link.rs` index it directly. Re-pointing and undo keep it in sync.
- Every existing caller keeps working, and the tests stay green.

## Appending (`network/append.rs`)

- `RoadStore::append_like(r, points) -> u32` copies road `r`'s columns (class, lanes, speed, layer and name, with the layer overridable) and pushes to every column.
- `NodeStore::append(pos) -> u32` pushes a node with control `Priority` and an empty incident list.
- A cut within 1e-6 m of an existing vertex reuses that vertex instead of inserting a duplicate, so no zero-length segment is created.
- `Network::append_*` keeps these structures in sync:
  - the node incident lists, which stay sorted
  - the `junctions` cache length
  - `RegionMask.active_road`, which gets `true` pushed, since the through roads were validated active
  - the `SpatialGrid`, fully rebuilt when a structural version counter changes, at the end of the edit and before the `Signals` rebuild, because `cluster::group` uses `nodes_within`
- `Network::set_endpoint(road, old_node, new_node)` removes the road from the old node's sorted incident list and inserts it, sorted, into the new node's. Apply and `UndoFlyover` both use it.
- `truncate_roads(len)` and `truncate_nodes(len)` undo appends.
  - They remove the truncated roads from the incident lists of their own `from` and `to` nodes.
  - They truncate `RegionMask.active_road` and the `junctions` cache.
  - They bump the structural counter.
  - They are only valid for the most recently appended ids. The undo stack is LIFO, so this always holds.

## `EditCommand::BuildFlyover { node: u32, through: [u32; 2] }`

### Validation (new `EditError` variants)

Checks run in M10a's order:
1. The node exists and has an active degree ≥ 3 (`NotAJunction`).
2. `through[0] != through[1]`, and both are active roads incident to the node and not loops, meaning `from != to` (`RoadNotIncident`).
3. Region mode: both roads must be active in the region (`OutsideRegion`).
4. **Straightness:** `d0` and `d1` are the departing directions of the two roads at the node, taken geometrically at 10 m via `departing(roads, road, node)`. That link may have 0 lanes, and `pose_at` clamps, so short roads need no special case. The angle between the two directions must be at least 150° (`FlyoverNotStraight`).
5. **Length:** each road has length ≥ 30 m (`FlyoverTooShort`).

Sort `through` ascending before use, so argument order never changes the ids.

### Effect, for each through road `r`

1. `d = min(80, length(r) / 2)`, measured from the node along `r`.
2. **Cut point:** the point at distance `d` from the node, inserted as a vertex in both pieces, so `len(outer) + len(inner) = len(r)`. Both pieces keep `r`'s point order:
   - **`r.to == node`:** the cut is at arc length `len − d`. The outer part is `points[..=k]` plus the cut point, and the inner part is the cut point plus `points[k+1..]`, where `k` is the segment holding the cut.
   - **`r.from == node`:** the cut is at arc length `d`. The inner part is `points[..=k]` plus the cut point, and the outer part is the cut point plus `points[k+1..]`.
3. Append a node `m_r` at the cut point.
4. Re-point `r` to its outer part, and move its endpoint at the node to `m_r` with `set_endpoint`.
5. Append the inner road `r'` with `r`'s class, lanes, speed and name, and the inner geometry. Its orientation mirrors `r`:
   - If `r` arrived at the node, `r'` runs `m_r → n'`.
   - Otherwise `r'` runs `n' → m_r`.
6. **Layer:** `1 + max` over `layer(r_a)`, `layer(r_b)`, and every road incident to a node within 1 m of the original node. A second flyover over the other pair therefore goes one level higher.

**Append order is fixed:** `n'` first, at the node's position, then for each through road in sorted order, `m_r` followed by `r'`. On `four_way`, with through `[0, 2]`, that gives `n' = 5`, `m_0 = 6`, `r_0' = 4`, `m_2 = 7` and `r_2' = 5`.

Afterwards:
- The original node keeps its other roads. Its degree drops by 2, and a degree-2 or smaller node loses any signal cluster.
- Bans at the node that mention `r` become inert, because `r` no longer touches the node. They are kept, so undo restores the exact set.

### Cost and inverse

- **Cost:** `2000 + round(10 × (len(r_a') + len(r_b')))`.
- **Inverse:** `UndoFlyover { node, roads: [r_a, r_b], old_ranges, old_lengths, old_ends, road_len_before, node_len_before }`.
  1. Handle vehicles first, as described under "Vehicles on undo" below.
  2. Restore both roads' ranges and lengths, and their endpoints via `set_endpoint`.
  3. Truncate roads and nodes to their previous lengths.
- **Changed ids:** the result reports the node, `n'`, both `m` nodes, both original roads and both inner roads.

### Afterwards

- Invalidate the junctions at the node, `n'`, both `m` nodes and both far ends.
- Signals rebuild, `version += 1`, and the route graph, demand tables and `LinkCosts` resize through the version check.
- Landmark tables keep their size; new link ids read as missing, which M7a already handles.
- **Vehicles on apply** run before M10a's generic vehicle pass, per through road `r`:
  - **On `r`'s arriving link** (toward the node): `s < len − d` stays on `r`. Otherwise the vehicle moves to `r'`'s arriving link at `s − (len − d)`.
  - **On `r`'s departing link** (away from the node): `s < d` moves to `r'`'s departing link at the same `s`. Otherwise the vehicle stays on `r` at `s − d`.
  - **Route splice:** a moved vehicle gets a new route with `r'` inserted right after `r` (arriving) or right before `r` (departing). The new route is appended to the arena, the old one counts toward `dead_len`, the cursor points at the `r'` entry, and the junction state is reset.
  - Lanes are unchanged.
  - The generic M10a pass then remaps movements at the node, strands vehicles whose movement is gone, and flags routes.
- **`UndoFlyover` runs in this fixed order:**
  1. Move vehicles off `r'` back onto `r`. Arriving: `s + (len − d)`. Departing: the same `s`. Vehicles on `r`'s departing outer link get `s + d`. Each moved vehicle gets a new route without the `r'` entry, through the arena as above.
  2. Restore both roads' ranges, lengths and endpoints (`set_endpoint`).
  3. Set `deleted = true` on both `r'`, then `version += 1`, then `ensure_graph`. A* can no longer route through `r'`.
  4. Strand vehicles in movements at `n'` or at an `m` node. Then, with no limit, reroute every live vehicle whose `route[cursor..]` still references an `r'` link. A failed reroute strands it.
  5. Truncate roads and nodes.
  6. Run M10a's generic vehicle pass using only the surviving ids: the node, `r_a`, `r_b` and their endpoints. The reported `changed_*` still list the truncated ids.

## Rendering note for M11

`networkDelta` must carry appended roads and nodes, plus re-pointed geometry. M11 handles this. M10b only reports the changed ids.

## Tests

1. **`flyover_bypasses_junction`:** on `four_way(2, 200.0)`, `BuildFlyover { node: 0, through: [0, 2] }` (north–south).
   - Road and node counts grow by 2 and 3.
   - Node 0 now has 2 active roads.
   - A vehicle north → south routes through `n'`, and its route never contains a movement at node 0.
   - A vehicle east → west routes through node 0.
   - With both streams running for 2,000 ticks, `check_invariants` holds, and every north–south vehicle's mean speed over its trip is at least `0.8 × v0`.
   - The expected ids are the fixed ones listed above.
2. **`flyover_undo`:** with north–south and east–west traffic flowing, apply the flyover, step 100 ticks, `Undo`, then step 200 ticks.
   - `content_hash`, road count and node count equal the originals.
   - The geometry of roads 0 and 2 equals the original point for point.
   - Node 0 has 4 active roads, and `ensure_junction(0)` has 12 movements.
   - `check_invariants` holds every tick after the undo.
   - No live route contains a link id ≥ `2 × road_count`.
3. **`flyover_errors`:** each new `EditError`, plus `NotAJunction` on a degree-2 node.
4. **`flyover_then_edit_undo_twice`:** flyover, then `SetLanes` on an inner road while a vehicle is on it, then `Undo` twice restores everything, and invariants hold.
5. **`flyover_region`:** in region mode, a flyover on an inactive road is rejected, and one inside the region succeeds with vehicles running.
6. **Determinism:** the M10a determinism test gains a flyover in its edit list, and two runs still match.
