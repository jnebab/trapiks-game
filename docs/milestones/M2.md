Goal:
- Areas (water and parks) go into the map format.
- The coastline becomes a sea polygon.
- A deterministic synthetic "grid city" is available to tests, benches, the wasm layer and the browser.

Generating the real Metro Manila map is deferred until `overpass-api.de` is reachable, which the orchestrator will run. Everything else here must be finished and tested now.

### `src/map/area.rs`

```rust
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq, Serialize, Deserialize)]
pub enum AreaKind { Water, Park }

#[derive(Clone, Debug, PartialEq, Serialize, Deserialize, Default)]
pub struct AreaTable {
    pub kind: Vec<AreaKind>,
    pub ring_start: Vec<u32>,
    pub x: Vec<f32>,
    pub y: Vec<f32>,
}
```

- `MapData` gains `pub areas: AreaTable` as its last field.
- **Ring layout:**
  - Ring `i` owns the points `ring_start[i] .. ring_start[i + 1]`, and `ring_start` has `ring_count + 1` entries.
  - A ring is stored open: the first point is not repeated at the end.
  - Every ring has at least 3 points.
  - Rings are drawn in table order, and water comes before parks.
- `validate` gains `validate_areas`:
  - `kind.len() + 1 == ring_start.len()`
  - `ring_start[0] == 0`
  - each ring grows by at least 3 points
  - the last entry equals `x.len()`, and `x.len() == y.len()`
- Accessor: `MapData::area_ring(&self, ring: u32) -> (&[f32], &[f32])`.

**`src/fixtures/grid_city.rs`:** `pub struct GridCity { pub cols: u32, pub rows: u32, pub spacing: f32 }` and `pub fn grid_city(spec: &GridCity) -> MapData`. The layout is fixed:

- **Nodes:** grid nodes at `(i · spacing, j · spacing)` for `i in 0..=cols`, `j in 0..=rows`.
- **Streets:** each column and row line is split into one road per block.
  - Line index `k % 5 == 0` is an arterial: `Primary`, 2 lanes each way, 60 km/h, named `"Avenue {k}"` for columns and `"Boulevard {k}"` for rows.
  - Otherwise it is a street: `Residential`, 1 lane each way, 20 km/h, named `"Street {k}"` for columns and `"Road {k}"` for rows.
- **One-way pair:** column lines 2 and 3 become one-way `Secondary` roads with 2 lanes. Column 2 runs toward +y and column 3 toward −y, and both keep their column name.
- **Signals:** every node where an arterial column meets an arterial row gets `Control::Signal`.
- **Skyway:**
  - A `Motorway` from `(−spacing, −spacing/2)` to `((cols + 1) · spacing, (rows + 1) · spacing − spacing/2)`, one-way, 3 lanes, 100 km/h, layer 1, named `"Skyway"`.
  - This offset keeps every skyway point off every grid node.
  - Its end nodes are new nodes, and it is split into 2 roads at its midpoint node.
  - It connects to no grid node.
  - The second half has no exit after the on-ramp. Vehicles leave the map at its end node, which is intended.
- **Skyway ramps:**
  - An off-ramp `MotorwayLink` (1 lane, one-way, layer 1) leaves the skyway's midpoint node and ends at grid node `(cols/2 + 1, rows/2)`.
  - An on-ramp `MotorwayLink` (1 lane, one-way, layer 1) leaves grid node `(cols/2, rows/2 + 1)` and ends at the midpoint node.
- **River:** a `Water` rectangle with x from `−spacing` to `(cols + 1) · spacing`, and y from `1.5 · spacing − 15` to `1.5 · spacing + 15`. Streets pass over it with no bridges modelled.
- **Park:** a `Park` rectangle with x from `(cols − 2) · spacing + 10` to `(cols − 1) · spacing − 10`, and y from `(rows − 2) · spacing + 10` to `(rows − 1) · spacing − 10`.
- **Counts:**
  - nodes: `(cols + 1)(rows + 1) + 3`
  - roads: `(cols + 1) · rows + (rows + 1) · cols + 4`
  - area rings: 2 (1 Water, 1 Park)
  - For 10 × 10 that is 124 nodes and 224 roads.

## `trapiks-mapgen` changes

### `osm.rs`

- `Member` gains `geometry: Option<Vec<LatLon>>`. Overpass `out geom` puts member way geometry inline and omits member node ids.
- Node members also gain `lat`/`lon` fields. Unknown fields are ignored by default, so never add `deny_unknown_fields`.

### `areas.rs` (new)

- **`area_kind(tags) -> Option<AreaKind>`:**
  - `natural=water` or `waterway=riverbank` gives Water.
  - `leisure=park|golf_course` or `landuse=grass|recreation_ground|cemetery|forest|meadow` gives Park.
  - Anything else gives `None`.
- **Closed ways:** a closed way (first node == last node, at least 4 nodes) whose tags give a kind becomes one ring.
- **Multipolygon relations:** a relation with `type=multipolygon` whose tags give a kind is assembled from its members:
  - Use members with role `outer` or `""` and a `geometry`. Ignore `inner` members until M15.
  - `join_rings(chains: Vec<Vec<LatLon>>) -> Vec<Vec<LatLon>>` joins chains end to end on exactly equal coordinates, reversing a chain when needed, until each ring closes.
  - Process chains in input order.
  - Chains that cannot be closed are dropped and counted.
- **Entry points:** tests call these directly, with an explicit `Projection`:
  - `pub fn closed_way_ring(way: &OsmWay, nodes: &BTreeMap<i64, OsmNode>) -> Option<(AreaKind, Vec<LatLon>)>`
  - `pub fn relation_rings(relation: &OsmRelation) -> Option<(AreaKind, Vec<Vec<LatLon>>, u32)>`, where the `u32` is the dropped chain count
  - `pub fn finish(rings: Vec<Vec<LatLon>>, projection: &Projection) -> Vec<Vec<(f32, f32)>>`
- **`finish`** does, in order:
  1. Project in f64.
  2. Remove the closing duplicate point.
  3. Run the ring Douglas–Peucker.
  4. Drop rings with fewer than 3 points or an area below 100 m².
  5. Convert to f32.

### `coastline.rs` (new)

- Entry point: `pub fn sea_rings(ways: &[&OsmWay], bbox: &LonLatBox) -> (Vec<Vec<LatLon>>, u32)`, where the `u32` is `dropped_coastline_pieces`.
- `LonLatBox { min_lon, min_lat, max_lon, max_lat }`.
- Input is the `natural=coastline` ways and the map bbox in lon/lat. Work in lon/lat with y up; project only at the end, via `areas::finish`.

1. **Join** coastline ways into maximal chains on equal endpoint coordinates, keeping each way's direction. Coastline ways are never reversed.
   - Ways are processed in ascending way id. When several ways could continue a chain, the lowest id wins.
   - A closed chain lying fully inside the bbox is an island. It is ignored, so islands render as water until M15; this is a known gap.
   - A closed chain that crosses the bbox is rotated to start at its first point outside the bbox, then treated as open.
2. **Clip** each open chain to the bbox with a Liang–Barsky helper per segment. The result is pieces.
   - Each piece records its `entry_edge` and `exit_edge`: which boundary it crossed, taken from the active Liang–Barsky parameter.
   - The crossing coordinate on that edge is snapped exactly to the edge value.
   - A piece with an endpoint strictly inside the bbox (the chain ends inside) is dropped and counted.
3. **Parametrize the boundary** by a perimeter position `t` that increases clockwise in lon/lat, starting at the top-left corner `(min_lon, max_lat)`:
   - The top edge runs to the top-right corner.
   - The right edge runs down to the bottom-right corner.
   - The bottom edge runs to the bottom-left corner.
   - The left edge runs up to the start.
4. **Build sea polygons.** OSM puts water on the right of the coastline direction.
   1. Start from the piece whose entry `t` is smallest.
   2. Follow the piece to its exit.
   3. Walk clockwise along the boundary, emitting every corner passed, to the next piece entry at or after the exit `t`, wrapping around. Compute `t` from the recorded edge.
   4. Follow that piece, and repeat until the start piece comes around again. Each such cycle is one Water ring.
   5. Mark each piece as used when it is followed, and start new cycles from unused pieces, in ascending entry `t`, until all are used.
   6. If the next entry belongs to an already-used piece other than the cycle's start piece, abandon the cycle, add its pieces to `dropped_coastline_pieces`, and continue from the next unused piece.
5. Project and simplify each ring.

### `simplify.rs` (new)

- `pub fn douglas_peucker(points: &[(f64, f64)], tolerance: f64) -> Vec<(f64, f64)>` works in projected meters. It is iterative, using an explicit stack, not recursive.
- For rings, let `far` be the point at the greatest distance from `points[0]`. When that distance is 0, drop the ring.
  1. Half 1 is `points[0..=far]`.
  2. Half 2 is `points[far..]` followed by `points[0]`.
  3. Simplify each half, then concatenate half 1 with half 2 minus its first and last points.
- Drop rings with fewer than 3 points, or with an absolute shoelace area below 100 m², after simplification.
- The tolerance is 1.5 m.

### `build.rs`

- `pub struct BuildStats { pub dropped_chains: u32, pub dropped_coastline_pieces: u32 }` derives `Default`.
- `build` becomes `build(osm) -> Result<(MapData, BuildStats), String>`, and returns `Err` when no road is kept.
- It fills `MapData.areas` in this order:
  1. Water rings: closed ways, then relations, then coastline.
  2. Park rings.
- Within each group, closed ways are ordered by way id and relations by relation id.
- The coastline bbox is the projection bbox (every kept road node) in lon/lat.
- The synthetic path passes `BuildStats::default()`.

### Synthetic output

- The CLI gains `trapiks-mapgen --out <file.bin.gz> --synthetic <cols>x<rows>@<spacing>`, for example `--synthetic 30x30@150`. It writes `fixtures::grid_city` through the same `write` and `stats` code.
- `stats.rs` prints the area ring counts per kind and the dropped-chain count.

## Scripts and data

- `scripts/fetch-osm.sh` (bash, `set -euo pipefail`, repo root):
  - Writes `data/osm/q{1..4}.json` with `mkdir -p data/osm`, then `curl --fail --retry 5 --retry-all-errors --retry-delay 30 -o "$out" --data-binary @- https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter`.
  - The NCR bbox is `south=14.35 west=120.90 north=14.79 east=121.14`, split into 4 quadrants at the midpoints.
  - Each query starts with `[out:json][timeout:900][maxsize:1073741824][bbox:{s},{w},{n},{e}];` and defines `area["ISO3166-2"="PH-00"]->.ncr;`. Its union has:
    - `way["highway"~"^(motorway|trunk|primary|secondary|tertiary|unclassified|residential|living_street|road|service)(_link)?$"](area.ncr)`. Mapgen filters the excluded service types and access tags afterwards.
    - `node["highway"~"^(traffic_signals|stop|give_way)$"](area.ncr)`
    - `relation["type"="restriction"](area.ncr)`
    - water ways and relations (`natural=water`, `waterway=riverbank`), filtered by `(area.ncr)`
    - park and grass ways and relations, filtered by `(area.ncr)`
    - `way["natural"="coastline"]`, which uses the global bbox
  - It ends with `out body geom;`.
  - `--print-query <n>` prints quadrant `n`'s query and exits without network access.
- `scripts/build-map.sh` runs `mkdir -p web/public/maps`, then `cargo run --release -p trapiks-mapgen -- --out web/public/maps/metro-manila.bin.gz data/osm/q*.json`.
  - With `--synthetic`, it writes `web/public/maps/synthetic.bin.gz` from `30x30@150` instead.
- Commit `web/public/maps/synthetic.bin.gz`, generated by `build-map.sh --synthetic`.
- `web/public/maps/LICENSE` is a short notice:
  - "Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0, https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/"
  - "The Trapiks map files in this directory are derived databases under the ODbL, except synthetic.bin.gz, which is generated and contains no OpenStreetMap data."
- `.gitignore` already has `data/osm/`.

## Tests

In `crates/mapgen/tests/`:

Add `multipolygon(id, &[(role, way_id)], &[(k, v)])` to `tests/support/mod.rs`. It fills member geometry from the way's node coordinates.

1. `closed_way_area`: a closed `natural=water` way gives 1 Water ring with the duplicate closing point removed. A closed `leisure=park` way gives 1 Park ring.
2. `multipolygon_outer_rings`:
   - Two outer member ways that meet end to end, with the second reversed, give 1 ring.
   - An `inner` member is ignored.
   - An unclosable single chain gives 0 rings and a dropped count of 1.
3. `coastline_single_piece`: a coastline running north to south straight through the middle of the bbox gives 1 Water ring covering the west half. The shoelace area is within 1 % of half the bbox area, computed in projected meters.
4. `coastline_corner_wrap`: a coastline entering on the top edge and leaving on the left edge, flowing so that water is to the north-west, gives a ring containing the top-left corner and no other corner.
5. `coastline_two_pieces`:
   - The bbox is lon 120.95–121.05, lat 14.55–14.65.
   - Piece A runs north to south at lon 120.975, from lat 14.66 to 14.54.
   - Piece B runs south to north at lon 121.025, from lat 14.54 to 14.66.
   - Expected: 2 Water rings of 4 points each. The first contains the top-left and bottom-left corners, and the second the top-right and bottom-right corners. The summed area is within 1 % of half the bbox area.
6. `coastline_revisit_is_dropped`: two pieces arranged so that the walk from one reaches the other's entry after it was used give a positive dropped count and no infinite loop.
7. `douglas_peucker`: collinear points reduce to their endpoints, a 1 m zig-zag at a 1.5 m tolerance collapses, and a 5 m spike survives.
8. `synthetic_cli`:
   - Runs the binary via `env!("CARGO_BIN_EXE_trapiks-mapgen")` with `--synthetic 10x10@150`, writing into `std::env::temp_dir()`.
   - Gunzips the output with `flate2::read::GzDecoder`, then decodes and validates it with `from_bytes`.
